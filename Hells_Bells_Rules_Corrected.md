# Hell's Bells

*A trick-taking game of exact contracts, mischievous imps, and infernal bells*

You are a lesser demon competing to become Hell's Master Bell-Ringer. Each round, you sculpt a hand, predict exactly how many tricks you will take, and use a distinctive imp power to keep your own promise—or ruin somebody else's. As the game progresses, you discard more cards and have more control, but mistakes become increasingly expensive.

## 1. Components and objective

The game can be played with a standard Tarot Deck and printouts for the Imp powers, or
with custom components.

- **56 Minor Arcana playing cards:** 14 each of **Wands, Cups, Swords, and Pentacles**.
- **22 Major Arcana round cards**, separated into **15 regular cards** (0–XIV) and **7 catch-up cards** (XV–XXI).
- **10 imp character cards**, each with a persistent ability; each player uses one imp for the entire game.
- Something to record bids, tricks won, penalties, and recovered penalty points.

The goal is to finish five rounds with the **fewest penalty points**. Everyone starts at **zero**; zero is best. Major Arcana cards govern rounds rather than being played in tricks.

Ace (low), 2–10, Page, Knight, Queen, King (high). Each suit has 14 ranks.

## 2. Five-round format

Randomly remove 1 card per player from the deck. Then deal the remaining cards evenly to the players, adding any excess to the originally discarded cards. 
In a four player game, each round begins with **13 cards dealt to each of four players** and **four cards left undealt**.  Each player discards an increasing number of cards **before bidding**.

| Round | Round-card deck | Cards dealt | Cards discarded | Tricks played | Penalty per trick off |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Regular | 13 | 1 | 12 | 1 |
| 2 | Regular | 13 | 2 | 11 | 2 |
| 3 | Regular | 13 | 3 | 10 | 3 |
| 4 | Regular | 13 | 4 | 9 | 4 |
| 5 | Catch-up | 13 | 5 | 8 | 5 |

Thus, the later rounds reward increasingly deliberate hand construction. **Round 5 is always the catch-up round.**

### Round sequence

1. **Reveal one Major Arcana card.** For rounds 1–4, draw without replacement from the 15 regular cards. For round 5, draw from the seven catch-up cards. Its special rule applies throughout this round; **its assigned suit determines trump**.
2. **Shuffle all 56 Minor Arcana.** Set aside one random card per player face down as a common undealt reserve; deal the other cards evenly to each player, adding any residue to the undealt cards.
3. **Discard.** Everyone privately discards two cards per round counter (that is, two cards in round 1, four in round 2, and so on).
4. **Resolve pre-bid abilities**, including any that alter cards or reveal information. Everyone finishes this stage before anyone bids.
5. **Bid publicly** in clockwise order, beginning to the dealer's left; the dealer bids last. Each player names the *exact* number of tricks they intend to win. Zero is a valid bid.
6. **Play tricks**, beginning with the player to the dealer's left. Normally the trick winner leads the next trick. Imp and Arcana powers can change the normal rules.
7. **Score**, update standings, collect all Minor Arcana, rotate the dealer clockwise, and begin the next round. Imp abilities refresh.

The **first dealer** may be chosen randomly. Deal two random imps to each player; they choose one
for the game and discard the other. The dealer on each subsequent round is the closest player to the 
    previous dealer who has the lowest score or is tied for the lowest (best) score.

### The dealer's hook

Normally the dealer **may not bid a number that makes the sum of everyone's initial bids equal the number of tricks available**. For example, with 12 tricks and earlier bids totaling 9, the dealer cannot bid 3. This guarantees that at least one player misses their contract.

**Exceptions:** The Moon suspends the hook, and Death deliberately changes how the hook relates to counted tricks; see those cards below. An in-play power that later adjusts a bid does not cause the hook to be checked again.

### Trick-taking rules

The leader plays a card; its suit is the **led suit**. Other players, clockwise, must follow the *current* led suit if they can. If they cannot, they may play any card. Ordinarily, the **highest trump** wins if any trump is present; otherwise, the **highest card of the led suit** wins. Only the winner scores that trick.

The designated trump suit is fixed for the entire round. Furfur and Strength can temporarily remove trump's *winning advantage* without changing the designated suit. Amdusias can change the **led suit during a trick**; this does **not** change trump.

## 3. Scoring

**Penalty for the round = round number × |tricks won − final bid|.**

An exact contract scores **zero penalty**. Both winning too many and winning too few tricks cost points. For example, in round 4, bidding 3 but winning 5 costs **8 points**. In round 5, missing by two tricks costs **10 points**.

Keep a running total of positive penalty points; **lowest total after round 5 wins**. Some Major Arcana effects forgive errors or remove earlier penalties; no recovery can reduce a score below zero. If players tie for the fewest points, they share the win.

Unless a card explicitly says otherwise, a trick means **one physical trick**. Death's final trick is special and counts twice for bidding and scoring.

## 4. The ten persistent imps

Each player keeps the same imp for all five rounds. **All imp powers are optional and usable once per round unless otherwise specified.** Powers are generally invoked **on the imp's own play** or **after that imp wins a trick**.

### 1. Amdusias — The Musician

**Discordant Chime — On your play, once per round.** Play **any card** from your hand, without needing to follow the current led suit, and declare **that card's suit to be the new led suit** for the current trick.

- Every player **still to play** must follow the **new** led suit if able.
- Cards already played stay where they are. A card played earlier can be eligible to win if its suit matches the new led suit.
- **Trump still wins normally**, even if the led suit has changed, unless another effect suppresses trump for this trick.
- This works when Amdusias is last to play, too: the led suit changes for determining the winner even though no one remains to follow it.
- Amdusias may use this to escape a suit, conserve a card, force a different follow-suit obligation, or deliberately win or lose a trick.

**Important:** Amdusias himself plays the card that establishes the new led suit. His powered play is exempt from card-choice restrictions, including ordinary follow-suit requirements.

### 2. Valefor — The Thief

**Sticky Fingers — After winning a trick, once per round.** Exchange **one deliberately selected card** from your remaining hand with **one randomly selected card** from one opponent's remaining hand.

### 3. Furfur — The Stormcaller

**Thunderclap — When playing your own card into an ongoing trick, once per round.** Suppress trump's winning advantage **for this trick only**. Ordinarily, the **highest card of the final led suit** now wins, even if trump cards were played. Furfur's actual card play must otherwise be legal.

The trump suit remains designated for the round; only its advantage in this trick is removed. If Amdusias subsequently changes the led suit, use the new suit when determining the winner.

### 4. Bael — The Invisible

**Now You See Me — On your play, once per round.** Play one legal card **face down**. Reveal it after everybody has played, before determining the winner. Follow-suit obligations still apply. If Bael leads face down, he truthfully announces the suit being led.

### 5. Dantalion — The Mind Reader

**Mind Probe — After winning a trick, once per round.** Secretly inspect **two randomly selected cards** from one opponent's remaining hand, then return them. If that opponent has fewer than two available cards, inspect only those available.

### 6. Agares — The Wanderer

**Come Back Here — On your own turn, before playing your card, once per round.** Choose a card **already played** in the current trick. Its owner takes it back and must play **a different legal card** instead.

When determining which replacement cards are legal, treat the returned card **as though it were not in the player's hand**. Thus, if it was their only card of the led suit, they can now play off-suit. The returned card remains in their hand afterward. Then Agares plays his own card.

If Agares makes the original leader change cards, the replacement establishes a new led suit; anyone still to play follows it. If no different legal card is available, the original card remains. This power is **not** an immediate reaction after someone plays; it can only be used when Agares's own turn arrives.

### 7. Shax — The Trickster

**Blind Luck — On your turn, once per round.** Choose **any other player who has yet to play** in the current trick. When that player's turn arrives, they must play **a randomly selected card from their remaining hand**.

**The forced card does not have to be a legal follow-suit play.** This ability deliberately overrides follow-suit and other card-choice restrictions for that forced play. It cannot target someone who has already played in the trick.

### 8. Balam — The Seer

**Foretelling — Before bidding, once per round.** Secretly inspect **three randomly selected cards** from one opponent's hand and return them. This is pre-bid information, in contrast with Dantalion's reward for **winning** a trick.

### 9. Seere — The Swift

**Jump the Queue — After winning a trick, once per round.** Choose **any other player** to lead the **next** trick instead of following the normal rule that the trick's winner leads. The completed trick's winner does not change.

### 10. Andromalius — The Investigator

**Caught Red-Handed — After *every* trick you win**. Publicly reveal **one randomly selected card** from an opponent's remaining hand. That card stays face up and known to **all** players until it is played. This is **repeatable**, not once per round; everyone benefits from the information.

## 5. Major Arcana: round rules and trump

Each round card supplies **two things**: a public special rule and a **fixed trump suit for that round**. Its special rule is known before discarding or bidding.

The rule is that **the Major Arcana determines trump**, with no turned-over playing card. In a printed deck, the trump suit would be shown on each round card.

### Regular Arcana (draw one in each of rounds 1–4)

**0 — The Fool · Fool's Luck.** Before bidding, each player may discard **one additional chosen card**, face down, and draw a **random replacement** from the undealt reserve. The player's playing-hand size does not change. This happens after the normal round discard.

**I — The Magician · Sleight of Hand.** **Before bidding and before normal discards**, each player may exchange **one chosen card from their hand** for a **random card from the undealt reserve**. The outgoing card goes into the reserve and is shuffled into it after the replacement is drawn.

**II — The High Priestess · Hidden Knowledge.** Before bidding, each player may privately inspect **two randomly chosen cards** from another player's hand, then return them.

**III — The Empress · Abundance.** Immediately after winning their **first trick** of the round, each player may **increase their bid by one**, provided it remains within the possible bid range. Declining at that moment forfeits the opportunity.

**IV — The Emperor · Royal Command.** The player leading a trick
may declare **Royal Command as they play their lead**. Every other
player must play **the highest card of whichever suit they legally
choose to play**. If they can follow suit, that means their highest
card of the led suit; if void, they may choose any permitted suit
but must play its highest card. The command applies to **all
followers**.

**V — The Hierophant · Sacred Order.** If unable to follow the led suit, a player **must play trump** if they have any. Otherwise, normal play applies; there is no requirement to overtrump.

**VI — The Lovers · Bound by Fate.** **Before bidding**, each player
secretly chooses another player. If both players **make their
contracts exactly**, the choosing player removes **two existing penalty points**.
Selections are revealed when scoring.

**VII — The Chariot · Full Speed Ahead.** The winner of each trick normally must lead their **highest-ranked remaining card** on the next trick. Equal highest ranks in different suits allow a choice.

**VIII — Strength · Might Makes Right.** The **highest card of the
led suit** wins, **regardless of trump**. If Amdusias
changes the led suit, use the **new** led suit.

**IX — The Hermit · Solitude.** Before bidding, each player may
secretly **set aside one card from their retained hand** for the
**final trick**. It cannot be played earlier, so that player's final
remaining card is the reserved card. Treat the reserved card as unavailable 
for earlier plays or random card selection.

**X — Wheel of Fortune · Fortune's Favor.** Any player who makes their contract exactly removes **one existing penalty point**. Scores cannot fall below zero.

**XI — Justice · The Scales.** Players who **overshoot** their bids pay normal penalties. Players who **undershoot** their bids pay **double** the normal penalty. Exact bids score zero.

**XII — The Hanged Man · Upside Down.** **As each trick's leader plays the lead card**, they announce whether the **highest or lowest eligible card** will win that trick. This is a fresh choice **every trick**, not a rule fixed for the whole round. Trump is still the eligible winning suit if present; otherwise use the led suit. A later Amdusias change to the led suit affects which non-trump cards are eligible, not the leader's high/low choice.

**XIII — Death · Sudden Ending.** The **last physical trick counts as two counted tricks** for its winner. If there are *T* physical tricks, there are *T + 1* counted tricks. Players may bid as high as *T + 1*.

**Important:** The dealer's usual hook is applied to the *T + 1* tricks.

**XIV — Temperance · Perfect Balance.** Once in the round, immediately **after winning a trick**, a player may change their bid **up or down by one**, keeping it within the possible range. The dealer's hook is not rechecked after an adjustment.

### Catch-up Arcana (draw one for round 5)

Determine standings **at the start of round 5**; these standings control any first-place, last-place, or bottom-half references throughout that round. 

**XV — The Devil · A Deal with the Devil.** Before bidding, the player in last place secretly chooses an opponent. If that opponent misses their contract, the trailing player removes accumulated penalty points equal to **half that opponent's round penalty, rounded down**. The trailing player's own round penalty still applies.

**XVI — The Tower · The Fall.** The player who began the round in **first place** incurs **double** their usual round penalty if they miss their contract. Everyone else scores normally.

**XVII — The Star · A Glimmer of Hope.** Players who began the round in the **bottom half** of the standings may miss their contract by **exactly one trick** without incurring that round's penalty. Missing by two or more costs the full penalty; a forgiven miss is not an exact contract.

**XVIII — The Moon · Veil of Uncertainty.** Bids are submitted **secretly and simultaneously**, and **the dealer's hook does not apply**. After all bids are revealed, the player in last place may change their bid **up or down by one**. It may be possible for everyone to make their contracts.

**XIX — The Sun · Second Sunrise.** Anyone who makes their exact contract removes **five** existing penalty points. A player who started the round in **last place** removes **ten** instead. No score drops below zero.

**XX — Judgement · The Last Judgement.** After the initial bids are made, the player in last place may change their bid **up or down by up to two**. The dealers hook does not apply.

**XXI — The World · The Final Reckoning.** After the final round, anyone who made their round-5 contract exactly may erase the **largest remaining penalty from one of their previous four rounds**. This cannot remove a penalty twice or take the score below zero.

## 6. Timing, interactions, and playtest rulings

These details keep games consistent; the core imp and Arcana effects above take precedence over ordinary trick-taking rules.

- **All card-changing or information powers intended for before bidding happen before anyone bids.** No ordinary character gets to swap cards after seeing other players' contracts merely because they are the dealer. The Empress, Temperance, and certain catch-up cards deliberately modify bids at their specified later trigger points.
- **Trump is fixed by the round card.** Changing the led suit with Amdusias is not changing trump. Strength and Furfur remove trump's winning privilege only for a specified trick.
- **Follow the current led suit.** If Amdusias changes it mid-trick, only players who have not yet played must follow the new one. Cards already played remain in place. The final led suit determines which non-trump cards are eligible to win.
- **Exceptions to legality are deliberate.** Amdusias may play *any* card when using Discordant Chime. Shax forces a random card from the target's whole available hand, **not just legal cards**. Agares, by contrast, requires a legal replacement as though the returned card were absent. A normal Furfur or Bael play remains legal.
- **No completed trick can be changed.** Agares acts on his own turn while the trick is in progress. Dantalion, Valefor, Seere, and Andromalius activate after winning a completed trick.
- **The Hanged Man plus Furfur:** if both apply to a trick, Furfur removes trump's special eligibility and the Hanged Man's leader-selected **high/low** choice applies to cards of the final led suit. This compatibility rule was not explicitly settled and is provisional.
- **The Hermit's reserved card** is unavailable to play, exchange, or select randomly until the final trick. On that trick it is the sole remaining card, so it must be played. This is an editorial interpretation necessary to make the reservation work.
- **Face-down or exposed cards:** Bael truthfully announces the led suit when leading face down; card legality can be checked on reveal. Cards publicly exposed by Andromalius remain known until played, even if transferred by Valefor (playtest ruling).
- **Random inspection and random exchange:** mix the eligible cards face down; no player may deliberately select which card is revealed or swapped. If a target has no available cards, a power requiring a card has no effect.
- **Recovery and ties:** all recovery effects have a zero floor. In a standings tie, tied first- or last-place players receive the stated benefit or burden (provisional). For The Star, rank players by penalties at the start of round 5; the two highest-penalty players qualify, **including everyone tied with the second-highest player**. If all four are tied, all four qualify (playtest tie convention). The World erases only an outstanding penalty amount, not an amount already forgiven by other effects.

### Decisions still to confirm before publication

The conversation fixed the **deck structure, five-round sequence, increasing discards, scoring, dealer hook, timing of imp powers, and trump source**. It did **not** explicitly settle (a) the exact suit assignment printed on each Major Arcana, (b) Ace-high versus Ace-low, (c) character selection and tie-breaks, or (d) fine-grained collision rules between rare simultaneous effects. The conventions in this file make a four-player playtest possible without presenting them as agreed design decisions.

Other player counts can be developed after playtesting the four-player version; using this rulebook's 13-card deal for a different number of players would not work without adjustment.

## One-page quick reference

**Round:** Reveal Arcana/trump → deal 13 each, reserve 4 → discard 1/2/3/4/5 → resolve pre-bid effects → bid clockwise (dealer hooked) → play 12/11/10/9/8 tricks → score error × round number.

**Imp triggers:** Amdusias *on play/change lead*; Valefor *after winning/exchange random cards*; Furfur *on play/ignore trump advantage*; Bael *on play/face down*; Dantalion *after winning/peek 2 random*; Agares *on own turn before play/force legal replacement*; Shax *on own turn/force a future player to play any random card*; Balam *before bidding/peek 3 random*; Seere *after winning/choose next leader*; Andromalius *after every win/reveal 1 random publicly*.

**Arcana:** 15 regular cards for rounds 1–4; 7 catch-up cards for round 5. **Trump comes from the Arcana**, never a turned-over Minor Arcana. **Death can allow everyone to make their bids.**
